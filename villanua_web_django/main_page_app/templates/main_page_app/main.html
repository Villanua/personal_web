Bachelor's Degree in Industrial Technologies Engineering

I studied Industrial Technologies Engineering at the University of Málaga, specializing in electronics and automation. This degree gave me a broad foundation in engineering, covering areas like mechanics, structural analysis, electrical engineering, fluid mechanics, and thermodynamics.

As I progressed, I focused more on robotics, automation, and programming. I worked with object-oriented programming (C#, Unity), perception systems (3D vision, computer vision, machine learning, deep learning), and programming in C++, Python, and Matlab. I also gained experience in electronics, instrumentation, and programming for both industrial and collaborative robots.

My Bachelor's Thesis, titled "Estrategia de colaboración humano-robot en cirugía endonasal" (Strategy of human-robot collaboration in endonasal surgery), was part of the National Research Plan project "Global Planner for a Robotic System for Anastomosis". I also contributed to the publication "Collaborative Robotic Assistant Platform for Endonasal Surgery: Preliminary In-Vitro Trials".

The CRANEEAL robotic system includes two robotic arms, a haptic teleoperation device, and force sensors, all programmed using ROS for seamless communication and precise surgical assistance. The modular design ensures that all components interact efficiently.

I designed an obstacle creation module to prevent collisions with critical areas, while virtual force feedback was integrated into the haptic device to guide teleoperated movements and improve safety during procedures.

I also developed a camera calibration system to parametrize the extrinsic parameters, using a custom piece designed in Fusion 360 and 3D printed. This ensures accurate mapping between the 3D world and 2D images for enhanced surgical precision.

For the human-robot interaction, I created a state machine based on an ontology. It manages two operating modes: autonomous and teleoperated. The system detects and transitions between these modes through computer vision and models state changes with Markov chains, adapting to the robot's movements for smooth performance.

Finally, I designed a web interface that integrates all these components, giving operators an easy-to-use platform for interacting with the system. The interface offers real-time feedback, monitoring of the robotic arm, and control over both autonomous and teleoperated modes, making the system efficient and user-friendly during procedures.

Master's Degree in Robotics and Automation

Master's Degree in Robotics and Automation

I pursued a Master's in Robotics and Automation at the University Carlos III of Madrid. During the program, I took courses in intelligent control, machine learning, computer vision, medical robotics, humanoids, and mobile robotics.

My Master's Thesis, "Simultaneous Localization And Mapping And Segmentation", focused on mobile robotics.

During my Master's, I developed a SLAM algorithm for both 2D and 3D environments, using point cloud techniques such as ICP and NDT to generate 2D and 3D maps through pose graphs. I implemented door and room detection using computer vision techniques, including watershed segmentation. To make the door detection more robust, I applied two different algorithms, ensuring more accurate identification. This approach allowed me to create topological maps from the generated geometric maps, enabling a more structured representation of the environment for autonomous navigation and mapping.

Professional Experience

Professional Experience

I've worked on a variety of robotics and AI projects, from industrial automation to service robots and autonomous vehicles. My experience spans programming, system integration, AI-driven applications, and deployment of autonomous systems.

I have programmed UR3 and UR10 robotic arms, integrating them with various devices such as elevators, horizontal conveyors, and both mechanical and suction grippers for diverse applications. Additionally, I developed a UR3 robot with ROS and a depth camera for waste detection and recycling.

I deployed an autonomous mobile robot (MIR) equipped with dual 2D LIDAR sensors on a corporate campus for workspace disinfection, implementing programmed point-to-point movements and sensor activation. I also participated in reviving a mobile robot (Summit XL), restoring its functionality and enabling precise localization using a 2D LIDAR and an RTK GPS. To enhance its autonomy, I implemented a Kalman filter to fuse data from odometry, LIDAR, IMU, and GPS. Additionally, I developed a web interface in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, connecting it to ROS via Rosbridge. The system includes an SQLite database to manage routes and actions such as sound activation, image capture, navigation, and map switching, all controlled via Python. I also created a mission-tracking algorithm in C++, using a state-machine model to automate inspection routes.

In the area of service robotics, I deployed Bellabot and Kettybot autonomous robots for water delivery in a corporate dining space. These robots feature 2D LIDAR and 3D cameras for obstacle avoidance and were also implemented in external company events. Additionally, I worked with the Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped robot for events and pipeline inspections.

I contributed to a Data Science project utilizing neural networks, Monte Carlo simulations, and KNN to predict economic trends in public tenders. As part of this project, I developed a Django-based web interface that allows users to adjust algorithm parameters.

In the field of Generative AI, I have applied advanced prompting strategies and flow engineering using GPT, Gemini, LangChain, and LangGraph. I have implemented reactive agents, self-reflection systems, Human-In-The-Loop methodologies, and database query agents. Additionally, I have integrated Knowledge Graphs into PostgreSQL, enabling hybrid search capabilities that combine structured and unstructured data. I have also performed fine-tuning of language models in GCP, AWS, and local environments, applying quantization and LoRA techniques to optimize a Deepseek R1 model for specific use cases. My role involved designing architectures, planning projects, and adapting solutions to client requirements. I have developed multiple projects, including a document analysis system capable of extracting structured information from uploaded files, and various document retrieval solutions that use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to answer questions based on indexed data. These solutions were customized for different clients, incorporating features such as database queries, reference question integration, multilingual support, and the use of documents as guidelines.

I developed a system for uploading and processing multimodal documents, including PDFs, Word files, Excel spreadsheets, and images, enabling the extraction of client-defined information. I utilized libraries such as Unstructured to handle complex document parsing, ensuring accurate data retrieval from checkboxes, tables, and embedded elements. The solution generated question batches for auto-completing specific forms, streamlining manual workflows and enhancing accuracy.

I designed advanced RAG systems for contextual question answering, incorporating mechanisms for disambiguation and multi-response generation to enhance answer accuracy. These systems utilize a comprehensive set of frequently asked questions to refine query interpretation and improve relevance. Additionally, they integrate Knowledge Graphs to expand document understanding and include agents capable of retrieving and processing information from databases dynamically. Embedding-based batch question generation and routing agents further optimize responses, enabling context-aware decision-making and automated execution of client-specific actions.

I designed a semantic document search engine that retrieves files based on their content, overcoming limitations posed by non-intuitive file naming conventions. The system processes and analyzes more than 3,000 documents, returning results along with their exact location within a SharePoint repository.
